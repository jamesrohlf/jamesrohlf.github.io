The same trick, on a map you already know

Thirteen hundred cities across the lower 48. Click one and the country expands about it — then click another, and that one becomes the center instead

On a photograph of the sky it is hard to be sure nothing is being slipped past you, because you do not know where those objects ought to be. Here you do. Click Chicago and every other city moves away from Chicago, Denver twice as far as Indianapolis because it started twice as far off. Click Denver and Chicago is the one that moves. Nothing about the map changed in between.

8%

Albers equal-area conic projection, standard parallels 29.5° and 45.5°. City positions and populations from the plotly sample datasets; state outlines from MappingAPI. Alaska and Hawaii are left out so the frame stays tight on the lower 48; Dot size grows with population, and only the fifty largest carry names, so the points stay readable once the expanded copy doubles them. The selection is the 1050 largest in the country plus the 28 largest in every state: ranking on population alone stacks dots on the coasts and leaves the northern plains blank, which would read as though the expansion had an edge. Every state is represented, the smallest entry being Devils Lake, North Dakota. States were assigned by point-in-polygon against the outlines.

What it does and does not show

The map is an analogy and it has a false note, worth saying plainly: a country is not expanding, and Chicago and Denver are not receding from one another. What carries over is the geometry. Multiply every separation by the same factor and each point sees everything else move away from it, at a rate proportional to how far off it was — that is Hubble's law, and it holds whether the points are cities or galaxies.

What the map makes obvious, and the sky does not, is that you cannot tell from the pattern which point was chosen. Every city sees the same thing. There is no privileged city, and no edge to run out to — the expansion is a property of the spacing, not of a place. That is the whole content of the statement that the Big Bang had no center, and it is why the question “where did it happen” has no answer of the form “over there”.
