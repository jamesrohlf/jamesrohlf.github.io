A box with no walls

A harmonic series invites an obvious analogy: a particle in a box, or an organ pipe. Standing waves, a fundamental and its overtones, kn = nπ/L. The analogy is worth taking seriously, because one half of it is exactly right and the other half is exactly backwards, and the backwards half is the more interesting one.

What maps. The box length is the sound horizon: L → rs, and the peaks of panel 3 really do sit on a ladder with rungs nπ/rs. Measured against the computed spectrum, consecutive peaks are spaced by 1.017, 0.991 and 1.014 in units of π/rs — the ladder is right to about one and a half percent. The troughs are real nodes too: in panel 3 they fall to 2×10−4 of the peak value, a line spectrum, just as a box would give. (The filled-in troughs of the observed Cℓ come from the Doppler term and from projection, neither of which panel 3 includes.)

What is backwards. There are no walls, and nothing is quantised in space. Every wavenumber exists, continuously; none is forbidden. The ladder comes from a condition in time: adiabatic initial conditions start every mode at rest with the same phase, and last scattering samples every mode at the same instant. The ones caught at full amplitude are those that completed a whole number of half-oscillations in the time available. It is selection by phase, not by eigenvalue — closer to a rank of pendulums of graded length, all released together and all photographed together, than to anything with a boundary.

So the box tells you where the peaks are, through rs. It cannot tell you how tall they are: a box's harmonics may carry whatever amplitudes you like, while the envelope here is set by the driving and the damping of the first four panels. That division is the cleanest way to hold the whole picture — the box sets the positions, the matter density sets the heights — and it is why θ∗ is pinned throughout this page: the box length is held fixed so that only the heights move.

6  ·  the same physics in real space: one point perturbation

Panel 5 is the box, run forward. The three waves at the top are the same three wavenumbers as panels 1 and 2, now drawn along a line in space rather than against time; each swells, collapses and turns inside out at its own rate, and the heavy curve is their sum. Below, every mode in the range is sampled at that same instant. Early on there is no comb at all — almost nothing has entered the horizon and had time to ring. As the sound horizon grows the teeth appear one by one and sweep toward smaller k, because the rungs are nπ/rs and rs is getting longer. The box is lengthening. At last scattering it stops, and what is left is panel 3.

The faint vertical lines are the ideal ladder nπ/rs. The teeth sit slightly to the left of them, by a constant offset of about 0.13π: the first peak lands at 0.85 of nπ/rs, the second at 0.94, the third at 0.96, the fourth at 0.97 — exactly the behaviour of a fixed shift in phase. That offset is the acoustic phase shift from radiation driving and from neutrino free-streaming, and it is the clearest single sign that the box analogy is a description and not a derivation. A box would land on the rungs.

Panel 6 is the same physics seen the other way round, and it is the picture worth carrying away. Instead of decomposing into modes, put down a single point overdensity in the early universe and watch what happens to it. The dark matter, which feels no pressure, stays where it is and grows. The photon–baryon fluid cannot: pressure drives it outward as a spherical sound wave, a shell expanding at a speed close to c/√3. When the photons decouple the pressure vanishes and the shell stops, stranded at about the sound horizon. The photons stream away and fade; the baryons are left in a shell, and the dark matter at the centre and the baryons in the shell then pull on one another until both carry both features.

The shell and the dashed line do not quite coincide, and the discrepancy is real rather than a drawing error. At last scattering the shell peaks a little inside rs, near 133 Mpc against 144, because it is a broad feature and the sound horizon marks the wave front rather than the crest. It then keeps creeping outward and settles at about 149 Mpc — outside rs — because the baryons are not released when the photons are. Photon decoupling, which sets r∗ and the CMB peaks, happens slightly before the baryons stop being dragged along, which sets rdrag and the galaxy ruler. The two scales differ by a few Mpc, and keeping them apart matters when the CMB and a galaxy survey are compared.

What survives is a small excess probability of finding matter at a particular separation — about 150 Mpc, a fixed comoving length frozen into the universe. In the CMB it shows up as the peak spacing. In galaxy surveys it is the baryon acoustic oscillation, the standard ruler. The slider makes the connection concrete: push the matter density up and the sound horizon shrinks, the shell in panel 6 freezes at a smaller radius, and the ladder in panel 5 spreads out. They are one measurement, made twice.
