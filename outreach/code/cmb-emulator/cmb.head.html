The CMB Power Spectrum

Six numbers fix the standard cosmological model. Every one of them leaves a distinct fingerprint on the microwave background — a peak that moves, a peak that grows while its neighbour shrinks, a tail that fades. Move the sliders and watch which is which, against what Planck actually measured.

This is the TT spectrum — the temperature autocorrelation, and only that. Planck also measured the polarization spectra, TE and EE, which are not plotted here. That matters in one place below: the near-degeneracy between τ and As looks worse on this plot than it really is, because polarization at low ℓ is what actually breaks it.

Measurements: Planck Collaboration, Planck 2018 results. V. CMB power spectra and likelihoods, A&A 641, A5 (2020), arXiv:1907.12875; parameter values from Planck 2018 results. VI. Cosmological parameters, A&A 641, A6 (2020), arXiv:1807.06209. Spectra retrieved from the ESA Planck Legacy Archive.
