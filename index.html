James W. Rohlf

Professor of Physics · known as Jim

Boston University · DESI

I work on the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument, which is measuring the expansion history of the universe by mapping where galaxies are.

DESI takes spectra of millions of galaxies and quasars at the Mayall telescope on Kitt Peak, 5,000 at a time through robotically positioned optical fibers. Each spectrum gives a redshift, each redshift gives a distance, and together they form the largest three-dimensional map of the universe ever made. Imprinted in that map is a standard ruler — the baryon acoustic oscillation scale, frozen into the distribution of matter when the early universe recombined. Measuring it at a range of redshifts traces how fast the universe was expanding at each epoch.

The collaboration’s results now favor a dark energy density that evolves with time over Einstein’s cosmological constant, and the same data bound the sum of the neutrino masses — a particle physics measurement made by surveying galaxies. Boston University is one of DESI’s participating institutions.

Before DESI I spent four decades on collider experiments: CMS at the LHC, where I was project manager for hadron calorimeter detector controls and for the jets and missing energy physics group; UA1 at CERN, which discovered the W and Z bosons; CLEO at Cornell, which found the Υ(4S) and the exclusive decays of B mesons; and Fermilab E260, which observed hadron jets from quark scattering. I also write undergraduate physics textbooks.
